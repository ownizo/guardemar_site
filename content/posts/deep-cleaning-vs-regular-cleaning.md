A closed home still gets dirty. Saharan dust settles on terraces, salt air films the windows, insects find their way in and a musty smell builds up in wardrobes. When owners arrive after months away, the first day is often spent cleaning rather than relaxing.

Not every home needs the same cleaning, and not every clean needs to happen on a fixed schedule. It helps to separate the types.

## Regular cleaning

Regular cleaning keeps an occupied home tidy: floors, bathrooms, kitchen surfaces, bins and bedding. In a second home, it is most useful while the owner, family or guests are staying, and in the days around their visits.

A fixed weekly clean in an empty house often adds less value than owners expect. Rooms nobody uses do not need repeated cleaning. What they need is airing, observation and occasional dusting.

## Deep cleaning

A deep clean goes further: inside cupboards and appliances, behind furniture, windows and frames, shutters, grout, extractor hoods, terraces and outdoor furniture. In the Algarve, typical moments are:

- before the first arrival of the season
- after a long summer of family and guests
- after building or maintenance work
- before a property is photographed or shown for sale
- after a calima episode that leaves fine dust everywhere

See [Saharan dust and calima on Algarve homes](/blog/saharan-dust-calima-algarve-homes/) and [keeping a home presentable while it is for sale](/blog/keeping-home-presentable-while-for-sale/).

## Post-works cleaning

Building dust gets everywhere and often needs specialist equipment. Agree with your builder whether a final clean is included, and if not, book one separately before the furniture is uncovered.

## Briefing a cleaner from abroad

- Write a short room-by-room list and mark what is out of scope, such as valuables, safes or personal papers.
- Say which products must not be used on specific surfaces, such as natural stone or wood.
- Agree how linen and towels are handled.
- Ask the cleaner to report anything unusual: damp patches, leaks, pests, broken items.
- Decide who holds the key and who opens the property.
