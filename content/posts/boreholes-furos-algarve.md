Many villas and rural properties in the Western Algarve have a borehole (furo) or well (poço). For garden irrigation, it can seem like an obvious answer to the cost and restrictions of mains water. In practice, groundwater is a regulated resource, and an existing borehole is not automatically a licensed one.

## Groundwater is licensed, not simply owned

In Portugal, the use of groundwater is subject to licensing by the environmental authorities, through the Portuguese Environment Agency (APA). That applies both to drilling a new borehole and to using an existing one. Buyers sometimes discover that a borehole on their land was never registered, or that its paperwork does not match how it is used.

Before relying on a borehole, ask your lawyer or a specialist to confirm its licensing status, and keep the documents in your property file.

## Licensing has recently reopened in some areas

During the drought years, licensing of new groundwater abstraction was frozen in several aquifer systems in the south. In 2026, after exceptional rainfall replenished reservoirs and aquifers, the government lifted those restrictions. Applications for new wells became possible again in previously frozen aquifers, including Querença-Silves and Almádena-Odiáxere. The latter lies in the Lagos area.

That does not mean every application will succeed, or that conditions will stay the same. Owners considering a new borehole should take specialist advice on the current position before commissioning any drilling.

## Practical points for a borehole you already have

- Know where the pump, pressure vessel and controls are, and how they are powered.
- Record who installed and maintains the pump.
- Check whether the borehole feeds only irrigation, or also the house or pool.
- If the water is used for anything other than irrigation, ask a specialist about testing.
- Make sure someone can switch the pump off if a pipe bursts or the garden is flooding.

A pump running dry or cycling constantly can fail. A burst irrigation pipe fed by a borehole can run for days without anyone noticing.
