In an occupied home, a tripped breaker is noticed within minutes. In a closed one, it may go unnoticed for weeks. By the time someone opens the door, the freezer has defrosted, the dehumidifier has stopped, the alarm has gone quiet and the router no longer sends camera images.

## Why a closed home is more exposed

A second home usually keeps only a few things running: a fridge or freezer, an alarm, a router, perhaps a dehumidifier or an irrigation controller. Every one of those depends on a single supply. When the power goes, so does everything the owner relies on remotely.

Common triggers include a storm, a faulty appliance, moisture in an outdoor socket, or a general supply interruption in the area.

## Signs that the power may be off

Owners abroad often notice indirectly:

- cameras or smart devices go offline
- the alarm company reports a power failure, if the system is set up to do so
- smart meters or energy apps show no consumption
- a neighbour mentions that the house lights did not come on

None of these confirms the cause. Someone still needs to visit.

## Decide in advance what a visitor may do

A property-care visitor can observe whether the main switch or a circuit breaker appears to have tripped. Whether they should reset it is a different question. Resetting a breaker that trips because of a fault can make the problem worse. The safest approach is to agree in writing:

- which circuits may be reset once, if there is no visible damage, water or burning smell
- which circuits must never be reset without an electrician
- what happens to the contents of the fridge and freezer

If a breaker trips again, the next step is an electrician. Read [hiring an electrician in Portugal](/blog/hiring-electrician-portugal-overseas-owners/).

## Reduce the impact

Simple measures help:

- empty the freezer before long absences, or accept that its contents are at risk
- label the consumer unit clearly, in English and Portuguese
- ask your alarm installer whether the system has battery backup and how long it lasts
- keep a record of which devices must stay on
