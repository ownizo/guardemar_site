A small leak in an occupied home is often noticed through sound, smell, a damp cupboard or a change underfoot. In a vacant home, the same warning can remain unseen.

## Damage is partly a question of time

Water can affect cabinetry, plaster, flooring and neighbouring units before it becomes visible from an obvious place. Even a slow toilet or under-sink connection may create expense if weeks pass before anyone enters.

## Visual checks should follow water points

A property visit should include accessible areas beneath sinks, around toilets, visible pipework, water-heater surroundings and signs of staining or unusual moisture. It is a visual process, not pressure testing or invasive plumbing inspection.

## Agree the response in advance

Owners should decide who may turn off water where safe, when a plumber can be called and what spending approval is required. Clear instructions save time when a report is marked urgent.

Photographs help an owner and contractor understand the initial condition. A follow-up visit can then document whether the visible issue has stopped or further work is needed.

The [Guardemar inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/) includes water and moisture categories, whilst qualified plumbers remain responsible for diagnosis and repair.
