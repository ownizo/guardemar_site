Ferragudo mixes village homes, riverside apartments and villas on the slopes above the Arade. Many are seasonal second homes: busy in summer, quiet for long stretches afterwards. The village can feel compact and looked-after. The properties themselves still need a documented inspection when the owner is abroad.

The useful distinction locally is not “Ferragudo versus everywhere else”. It is village access versus hillside systems, and what marine air does to shutters, metalwork and drainage while the house is closed.

## Village homes and compact access

Traditional village properties can have narrow approaches, shared walls and little external land. Parking, gate codes and the simple question of how a visitor actually reaches the door should be written down. A first inspection that spends its time finding the entrance is not yet a condition check.

Inside, the priorities resemble other compact homes: doors, windows, shutters, humidity, accessible plumbing and mail. Shared walls mean a leak or a persistent smell is not only the owner’s problem. A visual check can flag moisture; it cannot diagnose a neighbour’s pipework or a building defect behind plaster.

Riverside apartments may add condominium access, lifts, garages and building rules to that picture. Those arrangements should be in the property file so that a visit does not fail at the entrance.

## Hillside villas, gates and terraces

Hillside villas above the village typically add gates, terraces, pools and gardens. That is a different inspection. Exterior systems keep running after the owner leaves. Irrigation can dry a slope or wet a wall. Pool appearance can change between specialist visits. Terrace furniture and pots can move in wind.

A gardener or pool company may already attend. Their job is not to open every bathroom or look under the kitchen sink. Structured [holiday home checks](/holiday-home-checks-algarve/) give the owner one record across the whole property.

From safe ground, the visit can also note obvious drainage paths on a slope, displaced covers and whether gates still close as instructed. It cannot become a landscaping survey or a geotechnical opinion.

## Marine air and visible drainage

Ferragudo’s position on the Arade brings marine air. Shutters, external metalwork and window fittings deserve a consistent photograph. Early visual change is useful because it can be passed to a specialist while the owner is still away, rather than discovered on arrival.

Visible drainage matters after rain. Hillside and riverside settings can show water where it is not wanted around thresholds, terrace outlets or the base of external walls. A dated image is the start of a conversation with a contractor, not a finding that the waterproofing has failed.

After significant wind or rain, a [storm property check](/storm-property-checks-algarve/) can be arranged when access is safe. It remains a visual record. It is not emergency-service response, roof access or a guarantee of attendance after every weather event.

## Long vacant periods

Properties may be busy during summer and then unopened for weeks or months. That is when a current visual record earns its keep. The first documented visit is a baseline. Later visits are a comparison: a new ceiling mark, a changed pool level, a shutter out of line, moisture beneath a sink.

Use the same [inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/) each time. Frequency should follow the house. A village apartment is not a hillside villa with irrigation. See [how often to inspect an empty property](/blog/how-often-inspect-empty-property-portugal/) and [what happens to a villa empty for six months](/blog/empty-algarve-villa-six-months/).
