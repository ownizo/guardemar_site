Looking after a holiday home from another country is mostly a question of visibility. The home may appear fine when you lock the door, yet water, humidity, weather and normal wear continue after you leave.

## Begin with the property’s risk profile

A ground-floor apartment has different concerns from a detached villa with a pool, irrigation and several external access points. Record the main water shut-offs, electrical board, alarm instructions, air-conditioning units, appliance requirements and trusted contractor contacts. This becomes the property profile used during every visit.

## Inspect on a reliable schedule

A useful inspection is more than collecting mail. It should cover access, visible security concerns, leaks, damp, mould, electricity status, bathrooms, the kitchen and agreed exterior areas. Pool and garden condition can be observed, whilst maintenance remains with the relevant specialist.

Frequency depends on the property and season. Monthly may suit a straightforward apartment; a villa with multiple systems may justify twice-monthly or weekly visits. Read the [Guardemar inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/) and compare the [property care plans](/plans/).

## Document every visit

Photographs and a simple status make remote decisions easier. A report should show what was checked, what changed, any issue found and the sensible next action. A history of reports also helps distinguish a new stain or crack from an established condition.

## Prepare for arrivals and departures

Before travelling, arrange a visual property check, ventilation and agreed checks of water, power, hot water and air conditioning. After leaving, confirm access points and systems have been left as instructed. The goal is straightforward: arrive at your home, not a list of avoidable problems.
