## Begin with the property’s risk profile

A ground-floor apartment has different concerns from a detached villa with a pool, irrigation and several external access points. Record the main water shut-offs, electrical board, alarm instructions, air-conditioning units, appliance requirements and trusted contractor contacts. This becomes the property profile used during every visit.

## Inspect on a reliable schedule

A useful inspection is more than collecting mail. It should cover access, visible security concerns, leaks, damp, mould, electricity status, bathrooms, the kitchen and agreed exterior areas. Pool and garden condition can be observed, whilst maintenance remains with the relevant specialist.

Frequency depends on the property and season. Monthly may suit a straightforward apartment; a villa with multiple systems may justify twice-monthly or weekly visits. Read the [Guardemar inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/) and compare the [property care plans](/plans/).

No universal timetable fits every home. Consider how quickly a problem could develop, how much equipment sits outside, whether the property shares services with a condominium and how long it normally remains unopened. The guide to [inspection frequency for empty properties](/blog/how-often-inspect-empty-property-portugal/) explains the decision in more detail.

## Manage the main vacancy risks

### Water and plumbing

Know where the internal and external shut-offs are, how the property should be left and what action is authorised if visible water is found. Inspection points can include accessible areas beneath sinks, around toilets, near water heaters and beside visible pipework. These are observational checks rather than pressure tests or plumbing diagnosis.

### Humidity, damp and mould

Closed homes can develop stale air, condensation or visible mould, particularly in bathrooms, wardrobes, north-facing rooms and areas with limited air movement. A humidity reading provides context, but trends, odours, surface changes and photographs are often more useful than one isolated number. Read the detailed guide to [humidity and mould in Algarve homes](/blog/humidity-mould-algarve-holiday-homes/).

### Electricity and air conditioning

Record how the electrical supply, appliances and air-conditioning units should be left. A visit can confirm obvious power status and agreed indicators, but electrical diagnosis belongs to a qualified electrician. Air-conditioning can support humidity management in some properties, but settings and use should follow the equipment guidance and the needs of the home.

## Pools, gardens, security and weather

A pool company and gardener each have a specialist remit. A whole-property inspection adds an independent visual overview: unusual pool level or colour, dry irrigation zones, fallen branches, displaced furniture, gate condition and visible damage after unsettled weather.

Security checks can include doors, windows, shutters, gates and obvious signs of forced entry. They should complement, not replace, alarm monitoring and suitable locks. Coastal homes may also need closer observation of external metalwork, seals and shutters affected by salt-laden air and wind.

Post-weather visits depend on local conditions, safe access and operational availability. They provide photographic confirmation rather than a guarantee that every weather event will trigger an immediate visit. Guardemar’s [storm property checks](/storm-property-checks-algarve/) explain the scope.

## Keys, mail and contractor access

Use coded key storage and clear written authority for access. Avoid attaching a complete address to keys. Decide in advance who may enter, whether Guardemar should remain during attendance and what photographs or follow-up are expected.

Mail should be checked because accumulation can indicate vacancy and important-looking correspondence may need to be brought to the owner’s attention. Contractor access is most useful when it creates a record of attendance without suggesting that the keyholder certifies the contractor’s work. See [secure key holding](/key-holding-algarve/) and [contractor access](/contractor-access-algarve/).

## Document every visit

Photographs and a simple status make remote decisions easier. A report should show what was checked, what changed, any issue found and the sensible next action. A history of reports also helps distinguish a new stain or crack from an established condition.

## Prepare for arrivals and departures

Before travelling, arrange a visual property check, ventilation and agreed checks of water, power, hot water and air conditioning. After leaving, confirm access points and systems have been left as instructed. The goal is straightforward: arrive at your home, not a list of avoidable problems.

### Before leaving Portugal

- Confirm doors, windows, shutters and gates.
- Record water, electrical and appliance instructions.
- Remove waste and perishable food.
- Confirm pool, garden and cleaning arrangements.
- Update keyholders, alarm contacts and contractor details.
- Schedule the next property inspection before travelling.

### Before returning to Portugal

- Request a current report and photographs.
- Confirm power, water and hot water status where agreed.
- Check visible pool, garden and access condition.
- Allow time for a qualified contractor if a concern is already known.
- Use [arrival preparation](/arrival-preparation-algarve/) for a final pre-travel check.

## Turn observations into a maintenance plan

The value of repeated reports grows over time. They can show whether a stain is stable, an irrigation problem is recurring or external deterioration is becoming more visible. Use those observations to plan qualified maintenance rather than waiting for a failure during the next stay.

Keep annual servicing, condominium responsibilities, warranties and contractor contacts in the property file. Guardemar can coordinate access and communication, whilst the relevant specialist remains responsible for technical advice and workmanship.
