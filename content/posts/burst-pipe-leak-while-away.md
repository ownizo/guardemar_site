The message nobody wants is a photograph of water on a tiled floor. It may come from a neighbour, from the condominium, from a cleaner or during a scheduled inspection. What happens in the next few hours depends on decisions that should have been made long before.

## 1. Make the situation safe, where possible

If water is actively escaping and the main stopcock can be reached safely, turning off the supply usually limits further damage. Where water is near electrical sockets, appliances or the consumer unit, nobody should take risks: that is a matter for a qualified electrician, and in a genuine emergency, the emergency services.

Owners should know exactly where their stopcock is, and whether turning it off affects anything else, such as irrigation, a pool top-up or a heating system. Record this in the property profile. See [electricity and water in an empty home](/blog/electricity-water-empty-holiday-home/).

## 2. Photograph before anything is moved

Before towels, mops or furniture move, take wide photographs of each affected room, close-ups of the visible source if it is apparent, and any marks on ceilings, walls or skirting. Note the date and time. This record helps the plumber, the condominium and the insurer understand the starting point.

A simple [evidence file](/blog/evidence-file-vacant-algarve-home/) kept over previous visits makes it easier to show that the damage is new.

## 3. Call the plumber, with authority

A plumber can only act once they have access and know what they may do. If you have agreed a spending limit and a preferred plumber, this step takes minutes. If not, it can take days. Read [finding a reliable plumber from abroad](/blog/finding-plumber-western-algarve-from-abroad/).

## 4. Tell the neighbours or condominium where relevant

In apartments, water rarely stays in one unit. The administrator may need to know, and the flat below may already be affected. Early, factual contact usually reduces disputes later.

## 5. Notify your insurer promptly

Most home policies expect prompt notification and reasonable steps to limit damage. Check your policy for the claim procedure, any time limits and whether you should wait for approval before repairs beyond what is urgent. Keep invoices, photographs and the plumber's report. Read [water damage claims: how to document before the plumber arrives](/blog/water-damage-claims-documenting-algarve-home/).

## 6. Dry out and follow up

Water that has stopped can still cause problems. Humidity, mould and swelling joinery often appear later. A follow-up visit a few days afterwards records whether the area is drying and whether anything new has appeared. See [humidity and mould in Algarve holiday homes](/blog/humidity-mould-algarve-holiday-homes/).
