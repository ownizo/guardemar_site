Water damage is one of the most common reasons for home insurance claims. In a second home, it is also one of the hardest to explain, because nobody saw it happen. By the time the property is opened, the leak may have run for days or weeks.

What the owner can show matters. The quality of the record made in the first hour often shapes the conversation with the insurer.

## Safety and limiting damage first

Making the situation safe and limiting further damage comes before documentation, but the two can happen together. If the main stopcock can be turned off safely, it usually should be. Where water is near electrics, a qualified electrician or the emergency services should be involved. Read [burst pipe or leak while you are away](/blog/burst-pipe-leak-while-away-algarve/).

Most policies expect owners to take reasonable steps to limit damage. Delays caused by waiting to photograph everything should be avoided.

## What to record

- **Wide shots** of every affected room, from the doorway, before anything is moved.
- **Close-ups** of the visible source, if apparent: a pipe, a fitting, an appliance.
- **Ceilings, walls and skirting**, including stains, bubbling paint or swelling.
- **Floors**, showing how far the water spread.
- **Affected belongings**: furniture, rugs, appliances, boxes.
- **The date and time** of each photograph.
- **The condition of neighbouring units**, if water has reached them and access is possible.

A short written note of what was found, by whom and when, complements the photographs.

## Keep the evidence

Do not throw away damaged items or replaced parts until you know whether the insurer wants to see them. Ask the plumber to keep the failed part and to note the cause in the invoice or a short report.

## Show the "before"

Insurers often ask how long a leak was running and whether the damage is new. Photographs from earlier visits, taken in the same rooms, can show the previous condition. That is why an [evidence file](/blog/evidence-file-vacant-algarve-home/) built over time is useful.

## Notify promptly

Check your policy for the claim procedure and any time limits. Notify the insurer or your insurance intermediary as soon as reasonably possible. Ask whether you can proceed with urgent repairs, and whether the insurer will send an assessor (perito).

## Condominiums

In apartments, the origin of the water and who is responsible may be disputed. Involve the administrator early. Read [condominium insurance vs your own contents policy](/blog/condominium-building-insurance-vs-contents-policy/).
