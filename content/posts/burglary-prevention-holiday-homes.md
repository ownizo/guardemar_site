The Western Algarve is generally a calm place to own a home. Still, closed holiday homes have one obvious weakness: they look closed. Shutters down for months, post piling up and a garden that nobody tends all suggest that nobody is coming back soon.

Good prevention is rarely dramatic. It is mostly about making the property look cared for, making entry harder and making sure someone notices quickly if something happens.

## Make the house look occupied

- Avoid the "everything shut" look. Some owners leave a few shutters partly open, if that is safe and the house remains secure.
- Use timers or smart plugs for some lights.
- Keep the garden maintained, particularly the front.
- Do not let post, flyers and deliveries accumulate. See [mail, bins and deliveries when nobody is there](/blog/mail-bins-deliveries-empty-home/).
- Avoid announcing absences on social media.

## Make entry harder

- Check that all doors, windows, shutters and gates lock properly.
- Ask a locksmith about the quality of locks on external doors.
- Secure ladders, tools and garden furniture that could be used to climb.
- Keep side gates and garages locked.
- Do not leave keys in obvious places.

## Do not leave valuables behind

Jewellery, watches, cash, documents and small electronics should not stay in a closed holiday home. If they must, consider a properly fixed safe, and check your insurance. Guardemar never opens, inspects or holds credentials for safes.

## Detect and respond

An alarm or cameras can tell you something happened. They work best with someone who can attend. Read [alarm monitoring in the Algarve](/blog/alarm-monitoring-algarve-what-happens/) and [CCTV at your Algarve home: what Portuguese law allows](/blog/cctv-algarve-home-portuguese-law/).

## Know your neighbours

Neighbours who know you and have your contact details are often the first to notice something unusual. In condominiums, the administrator should have your current details.

## Regular visits

A property visited regularly looks visited. Doors are tried, windows checked and the post collected. Changes are photographed and reported. ## If something happens

If a break-in is suspected, nobody should enter the property alone. Contact the police, and avoid moving anything until it has been documented. Notify your insurer promptly. Read [questions to ask your insurer](/blog/questions-to-ask-insurer-second-home-portugal/).
