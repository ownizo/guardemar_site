Many second-home owners keep a car in the Algarve. It saves on rentals, makes airport runs simpler and means the beach is never far away. But a car that sits for months also has its own needs, both mechanical and administrative.

This is a practical overview, not legal or tax advice. The rules for Portuguese-registered and foreign-registered vehicles differ, and your situation may need specialist advice.

## The administrative side

For a car registered in Portugal, owners should keep track of:

- **Insurance.** Cars on the public road must be insured. Ask your insurer whether cover can be adjusted for long periods when the car is parked, and what conditions apply.
- **Annual vehicle tax (IUC).** It is due every year for registered vehicles, whether or not the car is used.
- **Periodic inspection.** Cars above a certain age need regular roadworthiness inspections. Missing one while abroad is easy.
- **Registration documents.** Keep copies in your property file, not only in the glovebox.

Foreign-registered cars kept in Portugal are subject to specific rules on how long they can stay and by whom they can be used. Owners in that situation should take advice from a specialist rather than relying on what neighbours say.

## The mechanical side

A car that is not driven for months can suffer from:

- a flat or failing battery
- tyres developing flat spots or losing pressure
- brakes seizing, especially in damp or salty air
- mould or musty smells in the interior
- fuel deteriorating over long periods
- animals or insects nesting in the engine bay

A mechanic can advise on the right preparation for your car, such as battery conditioning, tyre pressure and whether the handbrake should be left on.

## Where to keep it

A closed garage protects the car from sun, salt and dust, but can trap humidity. Outside, a breathable cover helps. In condominiums, check the rules for long-term parking. Read [garages and storage rooms in Algarve condominiums](/blog/garages-storage-rooms-algarve-condominiums/).

## Keys and access

Car keys left in the house should be kept securely and recorded. Anyone authorised to move the car needs your written permission and must be covered by your insurance.
