The white walls of the Algarve look effortless. Keeping them that way is not. Strong sun, salt air, wind-blown sand and occasional heavy rain wear down paint, render and woodwork faster than many owners expect, particularly in homes close to the coast.

## What wears out first

- **Paint on south- and west-facing walls**, which fades and chalks in strong sun.
- **Render**, which can crack and let water in, especially around windows and at the base of walls.
- **Woodwork**, such as doors, pergolas, shutters and window frames, which dries, splits and loses its finish.
- **Metalwork**, including railings, gates and fixings, which corrodes in salt air.
- **Garden walls and parapets**, which collect damp at the base and stain.

For the metal side, see [salt air, shutters and outdoor metalwork](/blog/salt-air-shutters-outdoor-metalwork-algarve/).

## Signs that work is due

Look for chalky paint that rubs off on your hand, hairline cracks in render, bubbling or peeling paint, dark staining at the base of walls and dry, grey woodwork. Damp patches inside, behind exterior walls, may also point to a problem with the render outside.

## Planning the work

- **Timing.** Spring and early autumn are usually best: dry, but not at the height of summer heat.
- **Quotes.** Ask for written quotes that list preparation work, products and number of coats. Preparation matters more than the topcoat.
- **Condominiums.** In apartments and many developments, façades are a common part. Changes and repairs may need approval from the condominium.
- **Water.** Pressure-washing walls before painting uses water and has been restricted in some drought periods. Check current local rules. Read [Algarve garden watering rules](/blog/algarve-garden-watering-rules/).
- **Scaffolding and height.** Work at height should be carried out by contractors equipped for it.
