A second home in the Western Algarve may be a lock-up-and-leave apartment or a villa with a pool, gates and irrigation. Both can sit empty for weeks. They do not need the same visit.

The mistake is to buy a “holiday home check” as if every address were the same, or to assume an apartment is automatically low-risk because it has no garden.

## What both property types share

Doors, windows, shutters, accessible plumbing, kitchens, bathrooms, obvious electrical status and the smell of the air matter in both. Mail, humidity in closed rooms and a slow leak under a sink do not care whether there is a pool outside.

A dated photograph of those areas is useful in either case. So is a written instruction for keys, alarms and who may authorise a contractor. The [inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/) is the common core.

Water remains the issue that grows with time. See [small water leaks in vacant homes](/blog/small-water-leaks-vacant-homes/). Humidity in unused bathrooms and wardrobes is the other shared theme: [humidity and mould](/blog/humidity-mould-algarve-holiday-homes/).

## Apartments: access, neighbours and the condominium

An apartment often fails at the entrance before it fails at a tap. Fobs, garage remotes, a reception or a condominium rule can block a visit that would have been simple at a private gate. Those details belong in the property file.

Inside, leaks can affect a neighbouring unit. That raises the value of looking under sinks and around sanitary ware, and of knowing how building-wide issues are reported. A home-watch visit does not replace the building manager. It records the owner’s interior and the parts of the exterior that are theirs.

Exterior cleaning, façade work and some shared systems may already sit with the condominium. The inspection should not assume that work has happened, and it should not duplicate a job the building already pays for without the owner saying so.

Mail accumulation is more visible in a stacked building. So is a bin store that nobody is using. Those are occupancy signals as much as housekeeping.

## Villas: systems that keep running outdoors

A detached house adds gates, terraces, outdoor furniture, and often a pool, irrigation and a garden. Those systems continue after the owner leaves. A pool company and a gardener each have a specialist remit. Neither is a whole-property inspection.

Irrigation can fail dry or wet. Pool level and colour can change between specialist visits. Wind can move furniture and shutters. Exterior metalwork and drainage are worth photographing from safe ground, particularly near the coast.

The visit still does not become a structural survey, a pool-chemistry service or work at height. It observes, records and, where authorised, calls the right professional. [Leaving a pool while you are away](/blog/leaving-pool-while-away-algarve/) and [irrigation during a long absence](/blog/irrigation-gardens-long-absence-algarve/) cover those outdoor systems in more detail.

## Frequency follows moving parts, not the postcode

A straightforward apartment may suit a monthly visit. A villa with several external systems may justify fortnightly or weekly checks, especially in unsettled weather. There is no universal timetable. [How often to inspect an empty property](/blog/how-often-inspect-empty-property-portugal/) is the better starting point than copying a neighbour.

Townhouses sit in between: some shared access, some outdoor space. Treat them as their own profile rather than forcing them into either label.
