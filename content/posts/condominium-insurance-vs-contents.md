Buying an apartment in Lagos, Portimão or Alvor usually means joining a condominium. Owners pay monthly fees, attend (or skip) annual meetings and assume the building is insured. Often it is. But that does not necessarily mean your apartment and its contents are protected in the way you expect.

This is a general overview, not insurance or legal advice. Check your condominium's policy and your own.

## Fire insurance is compulsory in horizontal property

Under the Portuguese Civil Code, buildings held in horizontal property (propriedade horizontal), the legal form used for most apartment buildings, must be insured against fire, covering both the individual units and the common parts. Many condominiums arrange a single building policy. Others rely on each owner to insure their own unit.

Ask the administrator which approach your building uses and request a copy of the policy.

## What a building policy may cover

A condominium policy typically focuses on the structure and common parts: walls, roof, façades, stairs, lifts, garages and shared installations. It may also cover the structure of individual units, depending on the policy. Additional risks, such as water damage, storms and liability, vary.

## What it usually does not cover

- **Your contents**: furniture, appliances, belongings.
- **Your own improvements**: a new kitchen, built-in wardrobes or upgraded bathrooms may or may not be included.
- **Your liability as an owner**: for example, if water from your apartment damages the unit below.
- **Items in your storage room or parking space**.

These are usually matters for your own home policy.

## The water-leak question

Leaks between apartments are among the most common causes of disputes. Was the source in a common pipe or in your private installation? Was your apartment the source or the victim? The answer affects which policy responds. Clear photographs and a plumber's note on the source help. Read [water damage claims](/blog/water-damage-claims-documenting-algarve-home/).

## Checks worth making

- Does the building policy cover the structure of your unit, and for what amount?
- Does it include water damage, and from which sources?
- Do you need your own buildings cover, or only contents and liability?
- Does your own policy cover your storeroom and garage space?
- Are there conditions about long periods without occupation?

## Keep the administrator informed

Make sure the administrator has your current contact details and those of your local keyholder. In an emergency affecting the building, they need to reach someone who can open your apartment.
