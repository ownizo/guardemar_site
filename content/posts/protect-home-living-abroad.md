Protecting a home abroad is less about one device and more about a dependable system. Alarms and sensors can be valuable, but they do not tighten a loose shutter, smell a drain or meet a plumber at the gate.

## Keep an accurate property file

Document keys, alarm instructions, water and electricity controls, equipment, warranties and contractor contacts. Update the file after changes. New owners can start with a [property handover](/property-handover/) to establish a baseline.

## Control access

Avoid keys labelled with a complete address. Decide who may authorise entry, how access is recorded and what should be checked after a contractor leaves. Informal key circulation creates uncertainty when an urgent visit is needed.

## Use scheduled human inspections

A regular visual check complements technology. It can identify visible water, moisture, damage, mail accumulation and changes around the exterior. Reports should include photographs and priority rather than a message saying only that everything “looks fine”.

## Keep specialist responsibilities clear

Maintain direct agreements with appropriate pool, garden, alarm and technical providers. A property care contact can coordinate them without implying responsibility for specialist workmanship.

## Plan travel days

Use [arrival preparation](/arrival-preparation/) before flights and a departure check afterwards. That turns each absence into a managed period rather than a gap in knowledge.
