Many overseas owners start with a neighbour who has a spare key. Others ask a house-sitter to stay for part of the year, or appoint a professional keyholder. These arrangements can all be useful. They are not interchangeable, and mixing them without a written record is how access quietly becomes unclear.

The practical test is simple. If something goes wrong on a Tuesday in November, who can enter, who will look, and how will you know what they found?

## What a neighbour can and cannot cover

A trusted neighbour is often the first person who notices lights, mail or an alarm. That informal presence has value. It is also limited. Neighbours travel, work and have their own properties. They are rarely paid to follow a checklist, photograph bathrooms or wait for a plumber.

A spare key left next door also needs the same discipline as any other key: it should not carry a complete address, and you should know who else might borrow it. Informal circulation is the usual way a “temporary” set becomes permanent.

If a neighbour is part of the plan, write down what they have agreed to do. Collecting a parcel is not the same as inspecting for leaks. The [key-holding guide](/blog/key-holding-portugal-overseas-owners/) explains why coding, authority and an access record matter even between friends.

## House-sitters look after occupancy, not a vacant period

A house-sitter is in the property. That changes humidity, mail, lighting and the obvious “lived-in” signs. It does not automatically create a maintenance record, a contractor process or continuity after they leave.

House-sitting also has a start and an end. The vacant weeks on either side still need a plan. A sitter who keeps keys after departure, or who lets trades in without a log, recreates the same access problem in a different form.

Where a sitter is used, treat the handover like any other change of occupation: a visual baseline when they arrive, and another when they leave.

## What professional key holding is for

A professional keyholder exists so that authorised access is possible when you are abroad. The point is not a spare key in someone else’s kitchen. It is a coded set, a defined list of who may enter, and a record of why the property was opened.

That record is what allows a leak to be investigated, a delivery to be accepted or a contractor to attend without the owner guessing who still has a key. Guardemar’s [key holding](/key-holding-algarve/) and [contractor access](/contractor-access-algarve/) services are built around that process. They do not replace an alarm, a neighbour’s goodwill or a house-sitter’s occupancy.

A keyholder who also inspects the home on a schedule is providing [home watch](/home-watch-algarve/), not a 24-hour concierge. Visits are planned. Emergency response depends on availability and is not represented as constant cover.

## Use more than one arrangement if the roles are clear

There is no rule that you must choose only one person. A neighbour can still watch for obvious activity. A sitter can occupy the house in summer. A professional keyholder can hold the working set and inspect during vacancy.

The arrangement fails when everyone assumes someone else looked, or when three people have keys and none of them writes anything down. Keep one property file: who holds which access, what a visit should include, and who may authorise a trade.
