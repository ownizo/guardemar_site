After a long, dry Algarve summer, the first serious rain often arrives suddenly. Gutters full of leaves, blocked terrace drains and cracked roof tiles all show up at once. In a closed home, water may come in for days before anyone sees it.

## Why autumn is the risk point

Through the summer, gutters and drains fill with leaves, pine needles, sand and dust. Terrace waterproofing dries and cracks in the heat. Tiles shift in the wind. None of this matters until the rain comes. Then, water that cannot drain finds the next way down, often through a ceiling, a terrace door or a wall.

## What to look at

A qualified roofer or builder should assess the roof itself. Owners can ask for:

- gutters and downpipes cleared and checked for flow
- terrace and balcony drains cleared
- flat roofs and terraces checked for cracked or lifting waterproofing
- loose, broken or slipped roof tiles
- flashings around chimneys, skylights and solar panels
- trees and shrubs touching the roof or walls

Many Algarve homes have flat roofs and roof terraces, which depend completely on drainage and waterproofing.

## Safety first

Roof work is work at height and should be done by people equipped for it. Guardemar does not access roofs or work at height. A visual check from the ground, terraces and inside the property can reveal signs such as staining, damp patches or water marks, but cannot confirm the condition of the roof itself.

## When to arrange it

September and October are the usual months, ideally before the first forecast of heavy rain. Builders and roofers become busy after the first storm, when urgent repairs take priority.

The first heavy rain is a useful test. A visit afterwards can show whether water has entered at windows, terraces or ceilings.
