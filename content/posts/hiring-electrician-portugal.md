Electrical problems in a second home are rarely dramatic. More often, a circuit keeps tripping, an outside light has stopped working, the water heater no longer comes on, or the gate motor is dead after a storm. Each one needs a qualified electrician. Each one is harder to arrange from another country.

## Electrical work is not a job for a handyman

Many owners have a general handyman who can hang a picture or replace a tap washer. Electrical work is different. Wiring, consumer units, earthing and outdoor circuits should be handled by a properly qualified electrician. Ask for a written quote, an invoice with your tax number (NIF) and, where the work requires it, the relevant documentation for the installation.

A property-care visit can record what is visible, such as a tripped breaker, a scorched socket or a damp consumer unit. It cannot diagnose the cause.

## Common issues in Algarve homes

Some electrical problems come up again and again in coastal second homes:

- moisture in outdoor sockets, garden lighting and pool equipment
- corrosion of exterior fittings in salt air
- trips caused by a faulty appliance, often a water heater or a fridge left running
- gate and garage-door automation affected by storms or power cuts
- older installations in village houses that were never designed for modern loads

For the salt-air side, see [salt air, shutters and outdoor metalwork](/blog/salt-air-shutters-outdoor-metalwork-algarve/).

## Prepare a short electrical profile

Owners can help any electrician by keeping a simple record:

- where the consumer unit is, with a photograph of the labelled breakers
- which circuits must stay on while the house is closed, such as the fridge, alarm, router or dehumidifier
- what can safely be switched off
- the installer of the alarm, solar panels or gate automation, if there is one

That profile is also what a property-care contact needs before deciding whether a breaker should be reset. If in doubt, the answer is to call the electrician.
