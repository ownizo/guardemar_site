Every spring, owners of rural properties in Portugal receive the same reminder: clear the land around your buildings before the deadline. For foreign owners, the rules can be confusing, and the work has to be arranged from abroad, often when contractors are at their busiest.

This is a summary for orientation, not legal advice. Your municipality and a local professional are the right sources for how the rules apply to your plot.

## What the rules require

Under Portugal's integrated rural fire management system (Decree-Law 82/2021), owners and others responsible for land near buildings in rural or forest areas must manage vegetation, known as fuel management (gestão de combustível). The best-known requirement is a strip of at least 50 metres around homes and other buildings in rural or forest areas. In agricultural land, the strip is narrower, at 10 metres.

Around villages and settlements next to forest areas, a wider strip of at least 100 metres applies, normally coordinated at local level.

Fuel management means cutting and thinning vegetation, removing dead material and keeping trees and shrubs away from walls and roofs. It does not mean bare earth everywhere. Properly maintained gardens are treated differently.

## Deadlines change each year

There is an annual deadline for the work, and it is regularly extended. In 2026, the government first set 31 May for most of mainland Portugal and 30 June for municipalities under a declaration of calamity. It later extended the deadline to 30 June across the whole country.

Do not plan around last year's date. Check the current deadline each winter.

## What happens if the land is not cleared

If owners do not carry out the work, the municipality can do it instead. Owners must allow access and repay the cost. Fines can also apply. Inspections focus on land around buildings, settlements and other sensitive infrastructure.

## Planning from abroad

- Find out early whether your plot falls within a fuel-management strip, and whether a neighbour's land does too.
- Book a clearing contractor in winter. Many are fully booked by April.
- Ask for dated photographs before and after the work, and keep the invoice.
- Agree how cut material will be removed or processed. Burning is restricted and may require authorisation.
- Do not forget access tracks, the area under trees overhanging the house, and gutters full of dry leaves.
