Security cameras have become cheap and easy to install. Many overseas owners put up a camera at the gate or on the terrace so they can check the property from their phone. In Portugal, that is generally allowed, but the rules on what a camera may film are stricter than many owners expect.

This is a general overview, not legal advice. For a specific installation, speak to a qualified installer or a lawyer familiar with data protection.

## No prior authorisation, but rules still apply

Since the European General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) came into force in 2018, private individuals in Portugal no longer need prior authorisation from the National Data Protection Commission (CNPD) to install a video surveillance system. That does not mean anything goes. The system must still comply with data protection rules and Portuguese law.

## Cameras must film your property, not other people's

The key rule comes from Law 58/2019, which implements the GDPR in Portugal. Cameras may not be placed on public roads, neighbouring properties or other places that are not the exclusive domain of the person responsible, except where strictly necessary to cover access to the property.

In practice, a camera at your gate should show your gate and drive, not the street, the pavement opposite or your neighbour's garden. Many owners adjust the angle or use privacy masking to blank out areas they are not allowed to record.

## No sound recording

Recording sound is prohibited for this type of system, except with CNPD authorisation in specific circumstances. Many home cameras record audio by default. Check the settings and turn it off.

## Inside the home

Cameras inside the home raise particular issues when other people are present: cleaners, gardeners, family, guests or contractors. They have a right to know they are being recorded, and some areas, such as bedrooms and bathrooms, should never be covered. If anyone works at the property, take advice before installing interior cameras.

## Signage and retention

Clear signs informing people that the area is under video surveillance are generally expected. Footage should be kept only for as long as necessary, stored securely and accessed only by authorised people.

## Practical checklist

- point cameras at your own entrances, not the street or neighbours
- switch off audio recording
- put up clear signage
- tell anyone who works at the property where cameras are
- keep login details secure and change default passwords
- check that cameras still work when the router or power goes off

## Cameras are only part of the picture

A camera tells you something happened. It cannot check for leaks, close a shutter or meet a contractor.
