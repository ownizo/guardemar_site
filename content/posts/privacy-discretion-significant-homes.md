Owners of significant homes in the Western Algarve often say the same thing: they want the house looked after properly, and they want as few people as possible to know about it. That is a reasonable expectation, and it shapes how a care arrangement should work.

## Fewer people, clearly named

Every person who holds a key, knows an alarm code or has the owner's travel dates is part of the picture. The aim is not to have nobody involved; it is to know exactly who is, and why. A short written list of authorised people, reviewed when someone leaves, does more for privacy than any single device.

## Keys without addresses

Keys should never carry the address of the house or the owner's name. Coded tags, kept separately from any list that links code to property, mean that a lost key is only a key. Every use should be recorded, so you can see who entered and when.

## Travel dates are information too

When the owner is arriving, when the house will be empty and when staff will be off are details worth protecting. Share them with the people who need them to do their work, and avoid broadcasting them in group chats with people who do not.

## Information about the house

Floor plans, alarm details, photographs of interiors and of valuable items are useful to the people looking after the house, and to nobody else. Ask anyone who holds them how they are stored, who can see them and how long they are kept. Under the GDPR you are entitled to clear answers.
