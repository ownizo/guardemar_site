Homes near the Atlantic do not only get a view. Salt-laden air, wind and sun act on shutters, gates, window fittings, terrace furniture and external metalwork. The change is usually gradual, which is why it is easy to miss if nobody compares this month with the last.

A visual inspection can record that change. It cannot restore a mechanism, repaint a railing or certify that a fixing is sound.

## Why shutters deserve a consistent photograph

Shutters are both weather protection and a security surface. A slat that no longer sits true, a motor that no longer completes its travel, or a panel left out of line after wind is worth reporting while the owner is still abroad.

The visit should follow the owner’s instruction: some shutters are left closed, some left open for airflow, some moved on a schedule. An inspector who finds a shutter in a different position from the last visit should treat that as a change, not as a styling choice.

Do not force a stiff shutter. A qualified technician deals with motors, tracks and seized parts. The report should say that the shutter did not operate as expected, with a photograph, not that it has “failed” as an engineering conclusion.

## Metalwork, gates and fittings

Gates, hinges, outdoor showers, stainless or coated railings, window handles and terrace furniture all take salt to different degrees. Staining, pitting or a gate that no longer closes as instructed are useful observations. They are not a corrosion survey.

Coastal locations such as [Sagres](/areas/sagres/), [Salema](/areas/salema/), [Praia da Luz](/areas/praia-da-luz/) and clifftop [Carvoeiro](/areas/carvoeiro/) make this a regular theme. Inland or more sheltered homes still have metal in the sun; the pace is often slower.

Painting, welding, replacement and work at height belong with the appropriate trade. Guardemar can [coordinate contractor access](/contractor-access-algarve/) and photograph the visible condition afterwards.

## After wind, look from safe ground

Shutters and lightweight furniture move in strong wind. Drainage on terraces shows whether outlets are clear. None of that requires climbing. [What to check after a storm](/blog/what-check-after-storm-algarve/) is the post-weather sequence; [storm property checks](/storm-property-checks-algarve/) is the service boundary, including the fact that attendance is not guaranteed after every event.

## Build it into the routine, not an annual surprise

Salt damage is a poor candidate for a single spring clean. A monthly photograph of the same gate, the same shutter bank and the same terrace railing is enough to see acceleration. That record also helps a contractor quote against a dated condition rather than a verbal “it has got worse”.
