Few subjects have caused as much confusion among Algarve homeowners in recent years as water. Between 2023 and 2025, drought led to a series of national and municipal measures, some of them directly affecting private gardens, lawns and pools. Owners who live abroad often heard about them late, through neighbours or news reports.

The situation in 2026 is different. Wet winters have refilled the region's reservoirs. But the rules are reviewed regularly, and the past few years show how quickly they can change.

## What happened during the drought

In February 2024, the government approved a resolution with restrictions that applied across the Algarve. Among them was a ban on using mains water, or drinkable water from other natural sources, to water gardens and lawns on private property, with limited exceptions such as keeping trees alive. Several municipalities, including Lagoa, Portimão and Faro, added their own measures for public gardens, fountains and pools.

For an owner abroad, the practical consequence was simple: an automatic irrigation system watering a lawn could put the property in breach of the rules without anyone noticing.

## Where things stand now

By mid-2026, the region's reservoirs were reported to be well filled, and day-to-day restrictions for residents and visitors had eased. The authorities have continued to review the drought situation at regular intervals, which means measures can return if conditions change.

Owners should not rely on what applied last year. Check the current position with your municipality (câmara municipal) or water supplier before each season.

## Practical steps for owners abroad

- Ask your gardener or irrigation installer to show you exactly what is watered, when and from which source.
- Keep a written note of the irrigation programme for each zone.
- Make sure someone can change or switch off the programme quickly if new restrictions are announced.
- Consider whether a lawn is still the right choice. Many owners are moving towards drought-tolerant planting that needs far less water.
- If the property has a borehole, understand the licensing position. Read [boreholes (furos) for Algarve gardens](/blog/boreholes-furos-algarve-gardens/).

## Pools

Pool filling and topping up have also been subject to restrictions in some periods. Before refilling or draining a pool, check what currently applies locally. See [leaving a pool while you are away](/blog/leaving-pool-while-away-algarve/) and [what a pool maintenance contract should include](/blog/pool-maintenance-contracts-algarve/).
