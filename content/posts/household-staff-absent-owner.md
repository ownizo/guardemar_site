A larger home in the Western Algarve rarely runs on its owner alone. There is usually a housekeeper, a gardener, a pool company, sometimes a handyman or a caretaker who has known the house for years. When the owner is in residence, this works naturally. When the owner is in London, Zurich or New York, it can quietly drift.

The issue is seldom bad faith. It is that nobody is accountable for the whole picture. The gardener sees the garden, the pool technician sees the pool, and the problem that sits between them — the irrigation leak that floods the pool plant room, the shutter left open before a storm — belongs to no one.

## Keep the people who know the house

Good staff and long-standing contractors are valuable. They know where the stopcock is, which shutter sticks and which neighbour to call. Replacing them with a single company is rarely the answer. What helps is a clear structure around them.

## Write down who does what

A one-page schedule removes most misunderstandings:

- who comes, on which days, and for how long
- what each person is responsible for, and what they are not
- who holds which keys and alarm codes
- who they call if something goes wrong, and who may approve spending

When this is written down, a missed visit or an overlap becomes visible rather than assumed.

## One accountable person on the ground

The role we play for owners of larger homes is simple to describe. We visit on a fixed schedule, see the whole house rather than one part of it, and record what we find. If the garden has not been watered, the pool looks wrong or a room has not been cleaned, the photographs show it, and you decide what to do.

We coordinate access and communication with your staff and contractors, pass you their quotes before any work is agreed, and record who attended and when. We do not employ, manage or pay your staff, and we do not give employment or tax advice. Those relationships remain yours, with your accountant or lawyer where needed.

## Access and keys

The more people who hold keys, the more important it is to know who has which. Keep an inventory, change codes when someone leaves, and avoid keys labelled with the address. [Smart locks, key safes and access codes](/blog/smart-locks-key-safes-access-codes/) covers the practical trade-offs.

## Before you arrive

The week before an owner arrives is when small failures show up: a pool that has turned green, linen not ready, hot water off. A [pre-arrival check](/blog/preparing-algarve-home-before-arrival/) a few days ahead leaves time to put it right with the people who normally do the work.
