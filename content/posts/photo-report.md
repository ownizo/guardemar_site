A useful report lets an owner abroad take a decision. A weak one says the house “looked fine” and leaves every later conversation starting from zero. Photographs are not decoration. They are how a new mark is distinguished from an old one.

This is what a Guardemar-style visit record is for. It is not a building survey, an insurance certificate or a claim that every concealed defect was found.

## Date, scope and who attended

The report should say when the visit happened, who entered, and which areas were included. If a room was locked, a roof was not safely accessible, or the pool cover was not to be lifted, that limit should be visible. A photograph of a living room is not evidence about a loft.

Access matters as much as rooms. If a contractor was let in on the same day, the attendance should sit in the same story. See [contractor access](/contractor-access-algarve/).

## Photographs that can be compared

The same viewpoints, visit after visit, are more useful than a random set of attractive images. Typical sets include:

- entrance, doors and a representative shutter
- kitchen, including accessible space under the sink
- each bathroom
- any room that previously had a mark or a humidity concern
- terrace, drainage points and outdoor furniture
- pool and a garden overview where those areas are in scope

A new stain has meaning when last month’s ceiling is on file. That is the same logic as the [inspection checklist](/inspection-checklist/).

Photographs do not prove hidden pipework is sound. They record what was visible on that date.

## A status, not a paragraph of atmosphere

Owners should be able to see what was normal, what needs attention and what is urgent. Those labels should attach to a specific observation: moisture under a particular sink, a shutter that would not close, a pool level clearly below the previous image.

Recommendations should be next actions, not diagnoses. “Ask a plumber to investigate visible moisture beneath the kitchen sink” is a report. “The pipe has failed” is a guess unless a plumber has said so.

## What was not done

A good report is explicit about its boundary. Visual, non-invasive, no roof access, no electrical testing, no pool chemistry unless a specialist did that work separately. That honesty is what makes the record usable later, including if an insurer or a buyer asks what oversight was in place.

## After a concern is raised

The follow-up should show that the owner was informed, whether a contractor attended, and the visible condition afterwards. Without that, the original photograph is a cliffhanger.
