A pool is usually the most expensive outdoor feature of an Algarve villa, and the one most likely to turn green while the owner is away. Most owners rely on a pool company. Few have a clear written agreement about what that company actually does.

## Typical services

A pool maintenance contract usually covers some or all of the following:

- scheduled visits, more frequent in summer and fewer in winter
- skimming, vacuuming and brushing
- water testing and chemical balance
- filter and skimmer cleaning, and backwashing where relevant
- a visual check of the pump and plant room
- reporting faults, leaks or unusual water loss

Repairs, replacement parts, chemicals and refilling are often charged separately. Ask.

## Questions to ask before you sign

- How many visits per month in each season?
- Are chemicals included, or invoiced separately?
- How will I know a visit took place? Photographs, a visit log or an app?
- Who decides whether to cover the pool, lower the water or put it into winter mode?
- What happens if the pump fails? Who can approve the repair, and up to what amount?
- Does the company check the water level and report unusual loss?
- Who has the key or gate code, and is the plant room locked?

## Water loss and leaks

Evaporation in the Algarve summer is significant, but persistent loss can indicate a leak. Pool leaks are a specialist matter. Early reporting gives the owner time to arrange detection before the problem affects surrounding paving or foundations.

## Water rules

Pool filling and topping up have been restricted in some periods. Before draining or refilling, check what currently applies with your municipality. Read [Algarve garden watering rules](/blog/algarve-garden-watering-rules/).

## Safety

If children or guests use the pool, ask the company to report damaged covers, fencing, alarms or lighting. Fencing and covers are part of safe use, not decoration.
